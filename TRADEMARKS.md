# OpenReview Trademark Policy

OpenReview is an open-source project dedicated to improving how developers and teams review code.

We encourage everyone to use, modify, distribute, contribute to, and build upon OpenReview in accordance with its open-source license.

This trademark policy exists to protect the identity of the OpenReview project, prevent confusion about official and unofficial versions, and ensure that the community can confidently identify software and services associated with the project.

## 1. Project Identity

The following are referred to as the "OpenReview Marks" in this policy:

- The **OpenReview** name, when used to identify this project.
- The official OpenReview logo, icons, and distinctive brand graphics.
- Other distinctive branding elements adopted by the project to identify its software or services.

This policy applies to the extent that these elements are protected by applicable trademark law.

## 2. Permitted Uses

You may use the OpenReview name without requesting permission to:

- Refer to the OpenReview project in documentation, articles, tutorials, presentations, and discussions.
- State that your software or service integrates with, supports, or is compatible with OpenReview.
- Identify the original project when distributing modified versions or forks.
- Link to the official OpenReview website or GitHub repository.
- Describe your contributions to or involvement with the project.

Such uses must be accurate and must not imply official endorsement, sponsorship, or affiliation where none exists.

For example, descriptions such as "Built using OpenReview" or "A community-maintained fork of OpenReview" are generally acceptable when accurate.

## 3. Uses Requiring Permission

Unless otherwise permitted by applicable law, prior written permission is required to:

- Use the OpenReview name or logo as the primary branding of an independently operated product or commercial service.
- Use the official OpenReview logo to represent an unofficial fork, distribution, or hosted offering.
- Create branding that is confusingly similar to the official OpenReview identity.
- Represent an independently maintained version as an official OpenReview release.
- Use OpenReview branding in advertising or merchandise in a manner suggesting official sponsorship or endorsement.

Permission may be granted for community initiatives, integrations, events, and other activities consistent with the project's goals.

## 4. Forks and Modified Distributions

You are free to fork, modify, redistribute, and commercialize the OpenReview source code subject to the applicable software license.

However, the software license does not itself grant trademark rights.

If you publish or operate a modified version of OpenReview:

- Clearly identify it as an independent fork or modified distribution.
- Avoid branding that could cause users to mistake it for an official OpenReview release or service.
- Use a distinguishable product name and visual identity when necessary to avoid confusion.
- You may accurately acknowledge that your work is based on OpenReview.

This policy does not restrict the rights granted by the project's open-source software license.

## 5. Official Branding

The official OpenReview name, logo, and visual identity are used to help the community recognize the project and its official releases.

Please do not alter or use these branding elements in ways that falsely imply a connection with the official project.

Use of OpenReview branding for legitimate commentary, comparison, identification, or other purposes permitted by applicable law is not prohibited by this policy.

## 6. No Implied Endorsement

Permission to use OpenReview source code does not imply endorsement, certification, sponsorship, or affiliation by the OpenReview project or its maintainers.

Third-party products and services should clearly distinguish themselves from official OpenReview offerings.

OpenReview's source code is distributed under the license specified in the repository's `LICENSE` file.

The software license governs the use, modification, and distribution of the covered software.

This trademark policy separately addresses uses of the OpenReview name and branding.

Nothing in this policy modifies, limits, or revokes rights granted under the applicable software license or applicable law.
